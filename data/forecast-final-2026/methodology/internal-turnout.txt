TURNOUT
What the turnout page measures

The Turnout page shows votes cast in every general election since 2016 — President, Senate, governor and House — by state, House district and county, as a share of citizen voting-age population, and estimates 2026 turnout for every Senate, governor and House race down to the county. Everything is computed on the server from the certified results already on the site plus one new input, CVAP.

DEFINITIONS
Turnout
Votes cast in the race ÷ CVAP. Votes, not ballots: no state reports ballots cast consistently, and blank or spoiled ballots add nothing to a race. VEP and total ballots from the U.S. Elections Project are shown beside the state figures as a cross-check.
CVAP
Citizens 18 and over from ACS table B05003 (scripts/fetch-cvap.py → data-entry/cvap.csv), one five-year release per election year (2016 → 2012–16 … 2024 → 2020–24; 2025 reuses 2024). Districts come on the Congress each release reports — the lines the election was run on. Connecticut's counties after 2021 are the 2021 release scaled by the state's growth, since the Census now publishes planning regions there.
First round
A race decided in a runoff or by ranked-choice rounds is counted at its first round (data-entry/turnout_first_round.csv, 15 races), the electorate that turned out; the race pages keep the decisive round. By-district figures for those races are the runoff round scaled to the first-round total.
Top race
The race with the most votes in a geography that year — the president in a presidential year; Senate, governor or the House total in a midterm. Drop-off is a race's votes as a share of it.
Unopposed seats
Shown as recorded (zero in FL, OK and LA, which tally no votes for an unopposed candidate) and flagged; the estimate imputes them as contested.
ESTIMATE · LEVEL
A state's top-of-the-ticket electorate

The 2026 estimate is a level × distribution model (lib/turnoutModel.ts). The level is set per state from its own midterm history; the distribution splits it across counties and districts.

votesS = CVAPS(2024) × Σy wy · rateS(y) · ticketS(y) · exp(slope × clamp(|m2026| − |my|, ±30))
rateS(y) = top-race votes ÷ CVAP in midterm y  ·  w2022 = 0.6, w2018 = 0.4
ticketS(y) puts the basis year on the target year's footing: ÷ 0.92 when the basis midterm had only House races and 2026 has a statewide race, × 0.92 the other way round. my is the basis year's top-race margin; m2026 the forecast margin of the state's closest Senate or governor race. Senate and governor votes are the level × the state's blended office-to-top ratio. The range is each basis midterm alone.

ESTIMATE · DISTRIBUTION
Counties

A county's share of its state's midterm electorate is its 2024 presidential share times its midterm propensity — how much more or less of the state's midterm vote it casts than of its presidential vote — blended over the basis midterms and shrunk toward 1 with 20,000 pseudo-votes. An office factor (the county's Senate-, governor- or House-to-top ratio relative to the state's, shrunk the same way) tilts it per office, and the state's counties are renormalised to the state total.

ESTIMATE · HOUSE
Districts

A district's estimate is the sum over its counties of (county estimate × the share of the county inside the district × the district's House drop-off). Drop-off is House votes ÷ the top statewide race within the district, contested seats only, so an unopposed seat is imputed as if contested.

Own drop-off
The district's 2022 figure, weighted 0.5 against the state's contested-seat mean, when the state has not redrawn since and the seat was contested. Redrawn districts and 2022's unopposed seats take the state mean; a state with no statewide race in a basis midterm takes the national mean.

PROJECTED RESULTS
County results and the implied generic ballot

The race pages' county maps (lib/countyProjection.ts) spread each race's forecast margin over its counties on the turnout estimate. The race margin is the forecast's, unchanged; the county figures are how it is expected to be made up. Adding every race back up gives the national vote the forecast and the turnout estimate imply together.

marginc = clamp(leanc + s, ±98)  ·  s solved so that Σc votesc · marginc / Σc votesc = forecast margin
repc = (100 − otherc + marginc) / 2  ·  demc = (100 − otherc − marginc) / 2
leanc is the county's TPL (its neutral-environment lean; the county's 2024 presidential margin where the county model has nothing to rest on); otherc its 2024 third-party share, capped at 8%. House counties are the district's pieces; a piece of a split county adds its 2024 deviation from the whole county (the piece's House margin minus the county's, same race, from the district-by-county results) where the lines are unchanged and every piece of the county was contested, so a county cut between a suburban and a rural district leans differently on each side. Redrawn states' pieces keep the county's lean. A decided race — one major party absent, or two nominees of the same party — is painted 100% for the side that has it rather than solved.

ELECTION NIGHT
The results needle

On /results the county projection above is the prior for a projected final margin that moves as counties report (lib/resultsNeedle.ts, run in the browser on every refresh of the civicAPI feed). Each county's counted votes keep their counted margin; its uncounted remainder starts from the prior plus a shared swing estimated from the counties that have reported, shrunk toward zero while little is in. The feed's race-level “percent reporting” is precinct-based and runs far ahead of the vote (in the real 2025 Virginia snapshots 70% reporting was 42% of the final vote), so completeness is measured against the turnout estimate instead. The needle never calls a race; calls are the feed's and lock the probability.

τ = (Σdone vc + kT) / (Σdone Ec + kT)  ·  the turnout factor from finished counties, kT = 0.1 × E
ec = max(τEc, vc / pc)  ·  fc = min(1, vc / ec)  ·  Tc = vc if done, else max(vc, ec)
s = Σ wc(oc − mc) / (Σ wc + kS)  ·  wc = vc fc, kS = 0.2 × E
rc = mc + s + fc2(oc − mc − s)  ·  Mc = (vc oc + (Tc − vc) rc) / Tc
σ² = (σforecast · √(kS / (kS + Σw)) · u)² + Σc ((Tc − vc) σcounty / T)² + (σlate · u)²  ·  u = uncounted share  ·  P(D) = Φ(−M / σ)
Ec and mc are the county's expected votes and margin from the projection above; vc and oc its counted votes and margin. pc is the feed's reporting figure for the county, used from 50% to under 100%. A county has caught up when its count is at least 90% of min(τ, 1) Ec: τ is statewide, so it may lower the bar but not raise it. A caught-up county is done at the feed's 100%, and from 90% its ec is vc / pc alone; only a county the feed sends no figure for can finish on the count alone, at 97%. House races use the district's county pieces, which the feed reports directly. Feed regions are matched to the priors by FIPS, else by name (Virginia's cities city-side, so Richmond City is not Richmond County); Rhode Island's towns, Chicago (reported apart from suburban Cook), Shannon SD and Kalawao HI add into their prior county, and a county listed at two levels (New England's counties beside their towns) counts once. Rows that match no prior county count as they stand, at their own margin. Where county detail covers under 60% of a race's expected vote, where unmatched rows hold over a quarter of the county rows' votes, or where the county rows' two-party vote runs more than 2% past the race's (the same votes listed twice), the same arithmetic runs on the race total alone. Where one ballot carries several candidates of a party (Louisiana's jungle round, and Alaska's and Maine's ranked-choice first choices), each side is a party bloc: the forecast's named candidate plus every Democrat, against every Republican, so the needle projects the party margin (for ranked-choice races, the final round) and the county map shows each county's own top two. Decided races keep the forecast. From 2026-11-06 05:01 UTC the race is in its late count, with two changes. civicAPI's percent stopped measuring what is counted, so a county or race below its last old percent keeps that percent until its count has grown 2% past its Nov 5 total. And the early-vote skew gives way to the late lean: the remainder runs oc + ℓ, with ℓ = (A (L − o₀) + k -8) / (A + k), A the votes the race has added since Nov 5 at margin L, o₀ its margin then, k = 30,000. Only a call then brings the sigma floor down to 0.3.

ELECTION NIGHT
The night model: what finished counties say about the races still out

The needle above reads each race from its own counties, so a race with nothing counted sat at its forecast however the rest of the country was running. Since 2026-11-01 the page first fits, once per feed refresh, every county that is at least half counted in any race (lib/resultsNight.ts): its swing against its prior is explained by terms the races share — a national swing carried into each state through the forecast's elasticity β, an offset per office, a term per state that the Senate, governor and House races in the state all see, and three slopes in the county's electorate. Each term is a Bayesian ridge with its own prior sigma, so a state with two counties in borrows the national swing and a slope stays near zero until counties of varied makeup are in. A race with nothing counted is projected at its prior plus the fit's shift for it, with the fit's standard error; a counting race starts its uncounted counties from that baseline and adds its own residual swing as before. Since 2026-11-02 the fit also learns each state's early-vote skew: the votes a county posts first are unlike its final count (the mail, counted first in most states, leans Democratic; where election-day votes come first the early count leans Republican), so a county's counted margin at completeness f runs about s (1 − f) from its final. Within each race, counties further along show less of it, so s is the within-race regression of a county's residual on (f − 1), over every county with votes: a national s pooled across states, and each state's own shrunk toward it. The needle reads a part-counted county's implied final as o + s (1 − f) and its remainder as o + s, instead of chasing the early count until the county finishes. The Results page shows the fit under the balance of power, and each race's footnote its share of it.

yc = oc − mc = βstate g + doffice + sstate + bnw xnw + bcol xcol + blean xlean + ε  ·  x = the county's share (or prior margin) less the national electorate's, per 10 pts
Σ = (XᵀWX / σc² + K)⁻¹  ·  θ = Σ XᵀWy / σc²  ·  K = diag(1/σg², 1/σd², 1/σs², 1/σb²)  ·  wc = min(1, vc / Vsat) once fc ≥ 0.35
shiftr = xrᵀθ at the race's vote-weighted average county  ·  ser² = xrᵀ Σ xr (+ σs² for a state with nothing in)
σown² = max(¼ σforecast², σforecast² − λ · prior-ser²)  ·  shared² = λ ser²  ·  λ = 0.5  ·  the needle's shared term becomes √((σown · shrink)² + shared²) · u
early vote: ec = yc − xcᵀθ, within each race ẽ, x̃ = (f − 1) demeaned by weight  ·  s̄ = Σ w x̃ ẽ / (Σ w x̃² + σc²/σm²) over every state  ·  sstate = s̄ + (Σ w x̃ ẽ − s̄ Σ w x̃²) / (Σ w x̃² + σc²/σm²) over its own  ·  the needle's oc becomes oc + s (1 − fc) in the swing and oc + s in the remainder
The baseline a counting race's uncounted counties start from is mc + shiftr plus the county's own demographic term beyond the race average; the race's swing s in the needle is then its residual against that. Decided races and races on their topline contribute no observations and take no shift. House pieces load their whole county's demographics. λ is below 1 because the fit also explains county-prior noise that is not the race's own miss: with all of the prior variance removed the 80% band covered 76% of races not yet counting on the replay; with half, 80%.

Nov 11, 2026
CHANGE
County House factor: a county is averaged over the midterms it has, not over both

A coding error, found in the post-election turnout review and fixed after the election at the user's direction because it was a mistake in the build, not something learned from the results. A county's House factor averages its House-to-top-race ratio over 2018 and 2022 (weights 0.4 and 0.6), counting a year only if every district touching the county was contested. The sum was divided by the weight of both years even where the county had one, so a county contested in 2022 only got about 0.6 of its ratio and one contested in 2018 only about 0.4 (Cook 0.40, Dallas 0.41, Tarrant 0.42, Manhattan 0.43, Nassau, Queens, Fulton, Allegheny, Broward and Palm Beach 0.61–0.64). Each county is now divided by the weight of the years it has. The same loop builds the Senate and governor factors, where no county was missing a year, so those are unchanged. The forecast does not read turnout: margins, ratings, probabilities and the lock are untouched (check-lock passes). The final-forecast archive (data/forecast-final-2026) was rebuilt with the corrected House votes and carries a note. Not fixed here: a few counties whose county House file is incomplete still carry low factors (Duval and St. Johns FL, Jackson MO, Hillsborough and Orange FL, Middlesex NJ), and Louisiana's run about 1.15.

EFFECT 139 counties in 12 states (TX 45, GA 27, PA 18, AL 14, SC 12, WI 11). 68 House districts move by more than 1%: IL-07 80,494 → 198,357 (191,697 cast), TX-24 93,779 → 226,470 (321,448), NY-12 102,369 → 245,207 (250,781). Projected national House vote 110,648,401 → 115,615,263 (115.8M counted on Nov 9 with California unfinished). Finished contested districts, published against the Nov 9 count: average miss 16.1% → 6.8%, lean +12.2% → +2.1%, within 10% 67% → 77%. The implied House popular vote moves about half a point toward Democrats (D+7.1 → D+7.6) because the cut counties were mostly Democratic cities.

Results night: shared swing fitted net of the early-vote skew; feed 100% needs half the vote; unopposed races settled; called races conditioned on the call

Three election-night changes to the results needle. (1) The night model's shared terms (national, state, office, demographic) had read each county's raw counted swing once it was MIN_COMPLETE counted, while only the per-race step removed the early-vote skew. CHANGE
Results needle: the early-vote term — the night model learns each state's mail/early skew and the needle reads part-counted counties with it removed (SIGMA_MIRAGE 3)

Across the realistic simulated night the projection of a counting race sat about a point Democratic of its final through the middle of the count (30–60% counted: mean −1.3) and closed only when the last counties finished: the votes a county posts first lean more Democratic than its final, and the needle extrapolated them. The Results page lists the national and state skews under "What early results say" and a race's footnote names its state's. CHANGE
Results needle: the night model — what finished counties anywhere imply for the races still out

The needle projected each race from its own counties alone, so a race with nothing counted sat at its forecast however the rest of the country was running. lib/resultsNight.ts now fits, once per feed refresh, every half-counted county's swing against its prior across all races to shared terms: a national swing carried into each state through its elasticity β, an office offset, a state term shared by the Senate, governor and House races in the state, and three slopes in the county's nonwhite share, college share and prior lean. Each is a Bayesian ridge with its own prior sigma (SIGMA_NATIONAL 3, SIGMA_STATE 2.5, SIGMA_OFFICE 1, SIGMA_NONWHITE 0.75, SIGMA_COLLEGE 0.5, SIGMA_LEAN 0.5; county noise SIGMA_FIT_COUNTY 6, full weight at VOTES_SAT 20,000 votes, in from MIN_COMPLETE 0.5 counted). A race with nothing counted is its prior plus the fit's shift for it, with the fit's standard error; a counting race starts its uncounted counties from that baseline and adds its own residual swing as before. The needle takes NIGHT_SHARE 0.5 of the terms' prior variance off a race's forecast sigma (all of it over-tightened the band). New scripts/resultsNightBacktest.ts replays the 2018–2024 Senate, governor and House county results in a simulated reporting order (poll-closing times, county ramps, an early-vote skew) and chose the constants on a grid; the Results page shows the fit (national swing, office offsets, slopes, states off their projection, the races not yet counting that moved most) and each race's footnote shows its share.

CHANGE
County TPL: each House year Huber-checked against the county's own statewide lean

A county's House figure carries its local candidates, and with no single incumbent to attribute (the county aggregate spans every district touching it) there is no incumbency strip to take them out. The gap entered at the full House weight: Cuellar won Zapata County, TX by D+34 in 2024 as Trump carried it by R+22, so Zapata's lean read D+6.4, and the border counties' projected Senate results came out 10–30 pts bluer than their recent record. A near-unopposed seat did the same in the other direction (Oktibbeha, MS: a 2024 House row of R+93 put the county at R+11.8 against a presidential R+0.3). County TPL now aggregates twice: once over the President, Senate and Governor rows alone to anchor the county's lean, then over every row with each House year's base weight × min(1, HUBER_C / |House NM − anchor|), the state model's House-year check with the county's own statewide lean as the anchor. No statewide row is Huber-weighted (a county far from its state is not an outlier). The projected county results on race pages read County TPL, so they move with it.

EFFECT Holdout (lean from the years before the target, one shift per state, vote-weighted RMSE across counties): 2024 President 5.84 → 5.18, 2024 Senate 4.69 → 4.16, 2022 Governor 4.11 → 3.80, 2022 Senate 4.02 → 3.77, county House 2024 6.97 → 6.90 and 2022 6.96 → 7.02. Sep 28, 2026
CHANGE
Turnout page and the 2026 turnout estimate

New /analysis/turnout: votes cast in every general election since 2016 (President, Senate, governor, House) by state, House district and county as a share of citizen voting-age population (ACS B05003, one five-year release per election year; scripts/fetch-cvap.py), with the U.S. Elections Project's VEP and total ballots as a statewide cross-check. Runoff and ranked-choice races count at their first round (data-entry/turnout_first_round.csv, 15 races; by-district figures scaled from the runoff round); unopposed House seats are shown as recorded and flagged. The 2026 estimate is a level × distribution model (lib/turnoutModel.ts): a state's top-of-the-ticket votes = CVAP(2024) × a 60/40 blend of its 2022 and 2018 top-race turnout rates, × 0.92 when its ticket gains or loses a statewide race, × exp(−0.0028 × the change in the top race's |margin|, the site's forecast margin for 2026); counties split it by 2024 presidential share × midterm propensity (shrunk with 20,000 pseudo-votes); House districts take their county pieces (2024 pieces, or tract CVAP in the ten redrawn states) × the district's contested-seat drop-off from the top race, so unopposed seats are imputed as contested.

EFFECT Backtest, 2022 from 2018 alone: state top-race MAE 8.1% (bias +6.2%, the 2018→2022 swing; 9.0% / +7.4% without the competitiveness term), county share of the state 4.4% vote-weighted with the level known, contested House districts 11.5%. 2026: 110.4M House votes (106.3–116.4M), 45.9% of CVAP, against 113.7M in 2018 and 107.7M in 2022.
