WAR · WINS ABOVE REPLACEMENT
How much better than a generic nominee

Borrowed from baseball, WAR measures how much a candidate added over a replacement-level nominee: a typical, non-incumbent candidate of the same party running in the same seat, in the same year, against the same opponent. It is counted in points of margin. A positive WAR means the candidate did better than that stand-in would have; a negative one means they did worse.

HOW IT'S BUILT
Three numbers
1
Expected result
What two typical nominees would have done in that seat that year: the place’s partisan lean, moved to that year’s politics, plus the national mood, incumbency, and the money gap two typical candidates would have had.
2
Expected against this opponent
A typical nominee does worse against a strong opponent and better against a weak one. Every candidate's strength is estimated from all the races they have run, and the expected result is moved by the opponent's. If the candidate is the incumbent, their incumbency is also taken out, because a replacement would not have been the incumbent.
3
WAR
The actual result minus the expected result against this opponent, counted toward the candidate. Positive is good for the candidate, whichever party they belong to.

QUESTIONS
Reading WAR
Why do both candidates in a race get credit?
Each candidate keeps whatever the race left unexplained, the way a batter and a pitcher both get credit for the same hit. So two opponents' WARs don't add up to the race's result.
Does money count?
Money a typical candidate would have raised in that situation is part of the expected result. Raising more than that counts toward the candidate.
Is it used in the forecast?
A closely related number is: the forecast’s candidate ingredient uses each nominee’s strength measured the same way, but with incumbency and all fundraising counted separately.
What are its limits?
Records start in 2016, so a long career before that is invisible. A candidate whose strength changed a lot over the years is measured with one number. A House member's own results are part of their district's lean, which slightly understates House WAR.
