STATE TPL · TRUE PARTISAN LEAN
A state's lean in a neutral year

State TPL is the margin a generic Republican against a generic Democrat would produce in a neutral national year. It is built from every eligible race from 2016 to 2025 — President, Senate, Governor, House and State Legislature, odd years included — by stripping three additive distortions from each and averaging across offices and years. It is the lean the Senate and Governor forecasts start from. Live ledgers: TPL tab.

margin(state, race, year) = lean + β*·E(year) + incumbency + fundraising + residual
NM   = Adjusted margin + incumbency strip + fundraising strip − β*·E(year)      each strip additive, applied once
TPL  = Σ year weight × WRS(year)        WRS = type-weighted mean of that year's NMs
Centered TPL = TPL − median of the 50 state TPLs
R-positive throughout. Candidate quality is deliberately not a term: outlier candidates are downweighted instead, and their residuals become WAR.

STEP 0
Which races count

A margin is a usable Republican-vs-Democrat measurement only if a genuine (or caucus-aligned) nominee of each major party was on the general ballot. There is no margin threshold: a legitimate blowout stays at face value.

CLASS	MEANING	TREATMENT
eligible	A real Republican against a real Democrat	Used as is
no-dem / no-rep	A major party absent — nobody, or a minor-party or unaligned independent stand-in (Osborn NE 2024, McMullin UT 2022)	Imputed
same-party	Top-two or runoff between two candidates of one party	Imputed
fragmented	Louisiana jungle general decided without a runoff: the top candidate of each party understates split fields. Detected as D% + R% < 90 in LA	Imputed
Aligned independents
Count as their party’s nominee: Bernie Sanders (D), Angus King (D), Kevin Kiley (R). Every other independent does not.
Write-in-scale slots
A slot under 5% while the opponent clears 90%, or under 2% outright, is treated as unfilled.
Margin basis
Every margin is R% − D% as a percent of the FULL total vote, third parties included — never a two-party share.
STEP 1
Adjusted margin and imputation
Eligible race
Adjusted = Raw, untouched.
Ineligible race (⊘)
Adjusted is imputed from the seat’s nearest presidential result: district-level for a House seat (restricted to the boundary vintage the race was run on), statewide otherwise. Imputed rows skip the incumbency and fundraising strips, strip the environment of the SOURCE presidential year, and enter aggregation at weight 0.5. An imputed House row instead keeps weight 1 × the source presidential year’s turnout share: it is that district’s share of the state’s House vote, and halving it would drop half the district out of the state’s sum.
State Legislature
Two rows per year over every chamber on the ballot that year, built district by district (Legislative atlas). The contested pool is the margin of the districts where both major parties drew votes, as a share of their full total, weighted by those votes. The unopposed pool (⊘) is imputed: each uncontested, one-party or uncounted district contributes its presidential margin on the map it was run on, weighted by that presidential vote scaled to the chamber's legislative-to-presidential turnout ratio; the row strips the environment of that presidential year. A district on the year's map with no results row at all (MEDSL's 2024 files and Louisiana's omit unprinted unopposed races) joins the unopposed pool when its seat was up — every seat, in a chamber that elects them all at once. A staggered chamber's seats not up join the contested row as estimates, so the row measures the whole chamber rather than whichever seat class was on the ballot (the classes can differ by several points in lean): each is its presidential margin plus the gap the contested seats ran from theirs that year (below). Before 2026-10-10 the entry was the raw chamber aggregate, which counted a printed unopposed race as a 100–0 result and so overstated the dominant party in every state that prints them. A year counts once every chamber that WAS on the ballot is sourced (staggered senates sit out some years; Nebraska is unicameral).
Senate specials
A state with a regular and a special Senate race in one year carries both rows; both feed that year's Senate mean.

Incumbency and fundraising strips

Each strip removes what the incumbent's party, or a money advantage, added to the margin — so what remains describes the seat, not the candidates' circumstances. The forecast adds incumbency back for 2026 from the same table.

STEP 4
The fit: environment, elasticity, incumbency

One national environment number per year, one elasticity per state, and the Senate and Governor incumbency values are estimated together by Huber-weighted alternating least squares over the full eligible panel.

adj  = margin + fundraising strip + incumbency strip   ≈   lean(state) + β(state) × E(year)
row base weight = RACE_TYPE_WEIGHTS[type] / count(state, year, type)
w    = base × min(1, HUBER_C / |residual|)                                 HUBER_C = 7
E(y) = Σ w·β·(adj − lean) / Σ w·β²  ×  n / (n + SPARSE_YEAR_K)             SPARSE_YEAR_K = 4
β*   = clamp(1 + BETA_SHRINK × (β̂ − 1), BETA_MIN, BETA_MAX)               = clamp(1 + 0.5(β̂ − 1), 0.5, 1.6)
inc(office) = Σ w·sign·(margin + FF strip − lean − β*·E) / Σ w            over incumbent-held Senate / Governor rows
4 alternating rounds (converged by round 4). The base weight makes a state-year's House rows share the House weight instead of outvoting its single presidential row, so the fitted lean answers the same question TPL does.

STEPS 5–6
Robust weighting and aggregation
aggWeight    = (imputed ? 0.5 : 1) × min(1, HUBER_C / |NM − fitted lean|)         statewide rows (P, S, G, Leg)
aggWeight    = (imputed ? 1 : 1) × total votes / mean House turnout that year     House rows — no row-level Huber; an imputed district keeps its share
aggWeight    = (imputed ? 1 : 1) × Huber × pool turnout / mean pool turnout that year   State Legislature rows — contested pool and unopposed pool share the L weight on their electorates
type NM      = aggWeight-weighted mean of the type's NMs that year
House factor = min(1, HUBER_C / |House NM − fitted lean|)      one Huber check on the whole House year, applied to its type weight
WRS(year)    = Σ type weight × type NM      base weights (House × its factor) redistributed among the types present
year weight  ∝ 0.75^(2026 − year) × coverage    coverage = Σ base type weights present that year (House × its factor)
TPL          = Σ normalized year weight × WRS
Coverage is what stops a sparse odd year (one governor race) from dominating through redistribution. A year with no races drops out and the rest renormalize. House rows are turnout-weighted because the districts sum to the state: each district's result is a share of the state's House vote, and a packed D+60 seat is not an outlier. Row-level Huber on House rows handed every packed-map state's House aggregate to the party with more middling seats (Georgia 2024: R+10 against a turnout-weighted R+2 and a presidential NM of R+0.3), so the outlier check is applied once to the whole House year instead. An imputed House row is weighted by the turnout of the presidential result it borrows, not by its own depressed turnout.

HIDDEN SEAT CLASSES · GENERATED 2026-10-23
Seats not up for election

A staggered chamber puts a different half (or third) of its seats on the ballot each cycle, and the seats up one year can lean several points either way of the chamber. The State Legislature row adds the seats that sat out as estimates, carried in that year’s own environment.

gap_j        = legislative margin − presidential margin           each contested seat up, on the lines it was run on
a, b         = vote-weighted fit of gap_j = a + b·(P_j − P̄)          P̄ = the contested seats' vote-weighted mean presidential margin
seat estimate = P_i + a + b·n/(n + 10)·(clamp(P_i) − P̄)    each seat not up; clamp = the range of P_j the fit saw
weight       = the seat's presidential votes × the chamber's legislative/presidential turnout ratio
row          = contested seats' votes + not-up estimates, combined on those weights
A chamber with fewer than 5 contested seats borrows the other chamber's gap that year. Which seats were not up: a staggered chamber's districts with no results row, except a seat whose last regular election was that year (an unprinted unopposed race, imputed as unopposed instead). OPEN ITEMS
Known limitations
State Legislature pools
A district with no presidential figure on its lines (New Hampshire's floterials, a key the boundary data lacks) is skipped rather than guessed; the environment fit still sees only the contested pool, since imputed rows never enter it.
House incumbency
Fixed, not fitted, in this state-anchored fit.
Tracking harness
The holdout does not re-fit E and β inside the window (the calibration script does); a strict refit-per-holdout harness is open.
No national TPL
The model is built state-up; the median state is the stand-in national baseline.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHEN
Revision history
Oct 19, 2026
CHANGE
State Legislature rows measure the whole chamber: staggered seats not up are estimated, unprinted unopposed seats imputed

A staggered chamber puts one seat class on the ballot each cycle, and the class up can lean several points either way of the chamber (Ohio's Senate alternated R+16 and D+4 presidential classes; Colorado's swung about 15 points between classes), so the row moved with the calendar rather than the voters. The contested row now adds every seat not up as an estimate: its presidential margin on the year's lines plus the gap the contested seats up that year ran from theirs, fitted on presidential lean (gap = a + b·(P − P̄), vote-weighted; slope shrunk by n/(n + LEG_NOT_UP_SLOPE_K) and P clamped to the fitted range; a chamber with under 5 contested seats borrows the other chamber's gap), weighted by its presidential vote × the chamber's turnout ratio. The estimate carries the year's own environment, so it joins the contested row, not the unopposed one. Separately, a district on the map with NO results row is now classified: in a chamber that elects every seat at once, or a staggered seat whose last regular election was that year, it is an unprinted unopposed race (MEDSL 2024 and the Louisiana files omit them) and is imputed like any unopposed seat — 69 Oklahoma House seats in 2024, 40–48 Louisiana House seats per year, 15 Florida and 16 Hawaii House seats, 13 Oklahoma Senate seats; MA Senate, VT House and NH House absences are left out because they match result rows the map keys miss. New: notUp summary in lib/stateLegChamberMargin.ts, notUpPool / legislatureRowPool in lib/chamberMargins.ts, LEG_NOT_UP_MODE "slope" and LEG_NOT_UP_SLOPE_K 10 in lib/tplCompute.ts (TPL_LEG_NOT_UP / TPL_LEG_NOT_UP_K override; CHAMBER_MARGIN_NO_ABSENT=1 drops the absent-district rule), scripts/stateLegNotUpBacktest.ts.

EFFECT Hidden-seat-class test (308 chamber-years that elected every seat, half the contested seats hidden): estimating the hidden half as the visible half, the old row, misses by 5.93 points (full-row 2.97); flat gap 2.86 (1.43); slope k 10 2.77 (1.38), k 5–10 best on both alternate and random splits. 2024 State TPL holdout flat within noise: P 2.24 → 2.24, S 4.90 → 4.91, H 3.41 → 3.42 (the row is 0.07 of a year). Live State TPLs: 13 states move 0.1 or more, mean 0.08 — Oklahoma R+30.5 → R+30.9, Wyoming R+42.1 → R+41.8, Louisiana R+20.0 → R+19.7, North Dakota, Alaska, Utah, Montana, Texas 0.2 more R, Colorado 0.2 less D. Displayed corrected chamber margins: Oklahoma 2024 R+24.8 → R+34.0, Florida 2024 R+19.8 → R+15.8, Louisiana 2023 R+31.2 → R+23.8, 2019 R+20.1 → R+18.7, North Dakota 2024 R+41.7 → R+40.5.

Oct 10, 2026
CHANGE
State Legislature rows rebuilt district by district: a contested pool and an imputed unopposed pool

The State TPL's State Legislature entry was the raw chamber aggregate — every counted district's votes summed — which counted a printed unopposed race as a 100–0 result and omitted an unprinted one, so it reported the margin of whichever seats happened to be contested and overstated the dominant party wherever unopposed races are printed. Each year now carries two rows that share the L weight on their turnout (lib/stateLegChamberMargin.ts): the contested pool (districts where both major parties drew votes, their own margin and votes) and the unopposed pool, imputed district by district from the presidential vote on the map the race was run on (data/stateLegPresByEra.ts, nearest presidential year), weighted by that presidential vote scaled to the chamber's legislative-to-presidential turnout ratio and stripping the environment of the source presidential year — the rule imputed House seats already followed. IMPUTED_LEG_ROW_WEIGHT = 1 (0.5 tested, no better). The environment fit is unchanged in form: imputed rows never enter it, so it now sees the contested pool. TPL_RAW_LEG=1 restores the old row for comparison.

EFFECT 2024 holdout: President 2.46 → 2.24, Senate 5.02 → 4.90, House average 3.06 → 3.41 (the harness references were re-baselined and the validation table re-emitted). 44 of 50 state TPLs move, 24 by half a point or more, mean 0.75, 34 toward Democrats: the states that print unopposed races compress toward their presidential lean — Wyoming R+44.7 → R+42.1, South Dakota R+30.8 → R+28.4, North Dakota R+35.0 → R+33.1, West Virginia R+36.2 → R+34.4, and on the other side Rhode Island D+20.3 → D+17.9 and Massachusetts D+30.0 → D+27.9.

Sep 22, 2026
CHANGE
Imputed House rows keep their full turnout share

With the state's House year taken as a turnout-weighted sum of its districts, an uncontested or same-party district entering at half weight dropped half of that district's voters out of the state's total. Imputed House rows now enter at IMPUTED_HOUSE_ROW_WEIGHT = 1 × their source presidential year's turnout share; their value is the district's own presidential lean, which is a better estimate of that share than a missing race. Imputed statewide rows stay at IMPUTED_RACE_WEIGHT = 0.5.

EFFECT Calibration objective 4.656 → 4.681 (0.75 would be 4.670); accepted so the districts still sum to the state.

Sep 21, 2026
CHANGE
House rows turnout-weighted, with one Huber check per House year instead of one per district

A state's House rows no longer carry the per-row Huber factor against the state's fitted lean. A packed D+60 district is a district, not an outlier, and the factor handed every packed-map state's House aggregate to the party with more middling seats (Georgia 2024: House aggregate R+10.1 against a presidential NM of R+0.3; 76% of all House rows were downweighted). House rows now enter their year's House mean weighted by total votes cast — the districts sum to the state's House vote — with imputed rows weighted by the turnout of the presidential result they borrow, not their own depressed turnout. The Huber check is applied once to the whole House year: its base type weight is multiplied by min(1, HUBER_C / |House NM − fitted lean|) before redistribution and coverage.
