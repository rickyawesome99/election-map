FORECAST · HOUSE, SENATE, GOVERNOR
One equation, three offices

All 506 races on the Forecast tab are projected by the same additive model. A structural Model margin is built from the seat’s partisan lean and four adjustments, then blended with the race’s Polling Average in proportion to how much polling evidence exists. All margins are R-positive: R+ is a Republican lead, D+ a Democratic one. House, Senate and Governor differ only in which lean they start from and in the per-office constants listed below.

TERM 1
Partisan lean (TPL)

The starting point is the seat’s True Partisan Lean: the margin a generic Republican against a generic Democrat would produce in a neutral national year. It is an absolute margin, not a lean relative to the nation. Senate and Governor races read the State TPL; House races read the District TPL, which is built on the 2026 lines.

Because TPL is built by stripping incumbency, fundraising and the national environment out of past results, the forecast adds the same three things back for 2026 with the same tables. A change to a strip therefore moves the forecast twice, in opposite directions, and mostly cancels; a change to the lean itself moves it one for one.

TERM 2
National environment

The generic ballot is not added to a race directly. It is converted onto the model’s own environment scale E, then multiplied by the state’s elasticity β*. Polling error is treated as uncertainty, not as a prediction.

WHY A CONVERSION
c and s fitted over 2016, 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024

E(year) is fitted jointly across every office and centered on the 2016–2025 average, so it is damped relative to the House popular vote: a one-point move in the House vote is worth 0.66 points of E, and E is zero when the House vote is D+0.9. Both numbers are properties of this model, so they are regressed from the model’s own fitted E against the actual House vote rather than assumed.

House races take the environment term times HOUSE_ENV_MULTIPLIER. On 2026-11-02 it was set to the House-vote scale (1 ÷ s, about 1.5 to 1.8) on the argument that the House popular vote is the sum of the House margins; on 2026-11-03 the forward backtest first scored 2018 and 2020 House races on the lines of the day (411 and 419 races instead of 6 and 5), and the clean sweep finds the House’s response to E year-dependent: at a multiplier of 1 the model ran 3 to 4 points too Republican across 2018 and about 0.8 too Republican in 2022, while 2020 and 2024 — the Republican polling-miss years — want less. Pooled error is nearly flat from 1 to 1.75; pooled bias rises with the multiplier. The value is a judgment between the year types and is recorded in the changelog with the sweep. The sum of the House projections sits below PV_hat whenever the multiplier is under 1 ÷ s; see Generic Ballot vs Popular Vote.

TERM 5
Candidates

Each nominee’s track record: how much better than a generic nominee of their party they have run before, net of lean, environment, incumbency and money. A nominee with no general-election record on file is a generic nominee (0).

Candidates pts = QUALITY_WEIGHT[office] × (effect_R − effect_D)
effect         = ridge candidate effect, solved as of 2026 over every scorable race 2016–2025
                 minimise Σ w·(r − a_R + a_D)² + λ·Σ a²        λ = 1
                 w = decay^(2026 − year)   decay 0.9 statewide · 0.8 House
r              = actual − (lean-in-that-year + β*·E + incumbency + FULL-money points)
The effects are the same machinery as WAR, with one difference: here the expected margin includes the full residual-basis money term, so that money stays a separate forward term and the effect does not contain it.

BLEND
Race polling

Where a race has general-election polls, their average is a second estimate of the same margin. It earns its share of the projection by evidence: no polls, the Model stands alone; many fresh polls, they dominate. 1,472 polls across 177 races are on file, and 177 races currently carry a polling average.

poll margin_i = (R% − D%) + aging shift_i − house effect_i
weight_i      = recency_i × √min(sample_i, 3000)         recency: 1 for 14 days, then halving every POLL_HALF_LIFE_DAYS[office]
Polling Avg   = Σ weight_i × poll margin_i / Σ weight_i   one poll per pollster (its latest)
nEff          = Σ recency_i                                effective number of fresh polls
w             = min(POLL_W_MAX, nEff / (nEff + POLL_K[office]))
blend         = (1 − w) × Model + w × (Polling Avg + POLL_MISS_SHRINK × POLL_MISS_HISTORICAL[office])
POLL MISS SHADE
Senate +1.65 · Governor +0.90 · House +1.25 (R-positive)

House effects are relative to the field, so a miss the whole field shares passes straight through them — and the field has shared one. The final polls (each pollster’s last poll from October 1, Polling Misses) have landed Republican of the result on average in every even year since 2016: Senate R+3.3 over 152 races, Governor R+1.8 over 101. The contested House vote has landed Republican of the final generic ballot in all five cycles, R+2.5 (Generic Ballot vs Popular Vote); with no House race-poll history on file, the House takes that figure. Since 2026-11-03 the blend reads each office’s poll average POLL_MISS_SHRINK × that miss toward the Republican — the same half-shade the generic ballot gets, applied once per race after house effects, whatever the pollsters. The displayed Polling Avg is the face value; the shade is shown beside it on the race page. The midterm misses (2018 ≈ 0, 2022 ≈ 1.7) were smaller than the presidential ones, which is one reason the shade is half rather than full.

lib/racePollAverage.ts
What is ingested
General-election polls naming both nominees, from the polling tables of each race's Wikipedia page. Partisan polls are kept and flagged (D)/(R): dropping or shifting them costs House coverage and accuracy.
Cycle window
Only polls that went into the field on or after 1 January 2026 count. Recency weighting had already left an off-year poll near-weightless, but it could still fill a race's table, stand as a pollster's latest survey, or hand a race with no 2026 polling a polling average of its own.
Pollster grades
Shown beside every poll but never a weight: a pollster's accuracy against the field has not carried into the next cycle (correlation 0.00).
Multi-candidate tables
Where a table lists several candidates of one party (pre-primary top-two, jungle), each party's candidates are summed and normalized to two-party.
Alaska
For all three Alaska races (Senate, Governor, AK-01) only a poll's ranked-choice FINAL ROUND between the two finalists (Wikipedia's ranked-choice table, round 3) is entered, by hand; a pollster's several final-round scenarios are averaged, and a poll with no two-candidate round is left out. First-choice toplines are never party-summed; the weekly scrape reads only the ranked-choice table for these races, and rows entered by hand from a pollster's own release (with decimals) are kept. AK-01's finalists are Nick Begich and the independent Bill Hill.
OUTPUT
Win probability, range and rating

A race’s error has two parts: a national miss shared by every race through β*, and noise of its own. The Model and the polling average err independently, so blending them narrows the race’s own noise; the national part is kept in full because polls miss nationally too. The split matters more than the total: a race’s own probability depends only on σ, but the seat ranges and the control odds depend on how much of σ is shared.

σ²            = (β* × σ_E)²  +  k² × [ ((1 − w) × RACE_SIGMA)²  +  (w × POLL_SIGMA)² ]
k             = THREE_WAY_SIGMA_MULT in a three-way race, else 1
P(D wins)     = Φ(−margin / σ)          clamped to 0.5% – 99.5%
80% range     = margin ± 1.2816 × σ

RULES
Races that are not one Democrat against one Republican

15 of the 506 races are decided by rule rather than by margin.

Uncontested
One major party fielded no nominee (a placeholder name on one side). Decided: probability pinned at 99.5%, rating Safe, displayed margin floored at ±20 toward the unopposed party, no simulation noise. Currently 6.
Same-party general
Both general-election slots belong to one party after alignment (California top-two, an incumbent-vs-incumbent pairing). Decided the same way. Currently 9.
Both nominees unknown
Stays contested and is scored structurally with no incumbency or candidate term (Louisiana's open all-party primaries).
Unaligned independents
An independent standing in for a missing party (Osborn in Nebraska and six others) stays contested: structural margin, their own track record, and polls. A win counts toward the slot's party in the seat totals.
Three-way races
When an independent holds a slot and that party’s own nominee is also on the ballot in double digits, the independent fills the slot and the nominee is a third candidate: Montana Senate is Kurt Alme (R) against Seth Bodnar (I), with Democrat Alani Bankhead third. Polls read the independent’s column and are never party-summed with the nominee’s; the race’s own error is widened by THREE_WAY_SIGMA_MULT. As with any unaligned independent, a win counts toward the slot’s party in the seat totals.
Louisiana House
All six seats are a Nov 3 all-party primary with a Dec 12 runoff. The modeled margin is the party-summed R-vs-D quantity, so an R-vs-R runoff is still the modeled Republican win.
Exact tie
If the blend lands on exactly 0 the side is taken from the Model, then the polls, then the pre-money lean.
AGGREGATION
Seat counts and chamber control

Summing win probabilities gives the expected seats but not their spread, because races miss together. Chamber totals come from 5,000 seeded simulations (the same seed on server and client, so the numbers never flicker).

simulated margin = margin + β* × z  +  load_nonwhite × z_nw  +  load_college × z_col  +  z_state  +  ε
z ~ N(0, σ_E)                         one national shock per run, shared by every race
z_nw ~ N(0, 1.5) · z_col ~ N(0, 1)       one shock per demographic axis per run
z_state ~ N(0, STATE_SHOCK[office])          one shock per state per run (House 3.5; Senate and Governor 0)
load = (race's share − average share of its kind) / 10      districts vs the 435, states vs the 50
ε ~ N(0, idio)     idio² = max( ½² × raceVar, raceVar − demographic variance − STATE_SHOCK² ),  raceVar = σ² − (β*·σ_E)²
The demographic and state shocks are carved OUT of each race's own noise, so a race's total spread and win probability are identical with or without them; only how seats move together changes. Decided races add their seat with no noise.

SERIES
Forecast over time

The Overview’s chamber charts and the Forecast Over Time chart on every race page come from the same series: the whole forecast rerun once per day from the first day the generic-ballot average rested on five pollsters to today, at every build.

Each day is today’s model on the data that existed then. The forecast clock is set to that day, so a poll counts only once its field period had ended, poll aging and pollster house effects are measured from that day, and the national environment’s horizon is that day’s distance to the election. Everything else is as it stands now: a later model change, map correction or candidate fix applies to every day, nominees are the eventual ones even before their primaries, and fundraising is the latest filing. It shows how the forecast moves with the polls, not what the site published on a given day. Today’s point equals the live forecast.

A race page plots the projected margin, signed toward the candidate favored by today’s margin so the line never flips mid-series, on a scale symmetric about even, and reads that day’s win probability into the caption. A race with no polls moves only with the national environment and the horizon. The series is written by scripts/buildForecastHistory.ts (npm’s prebuild and predev hooks) to data/generated/forecast-history.json.

SO THEY ARE NOT RE-TESTED BLIND
Tested, not adopted
Open-seat carryover
Points toward the outgoing holder's party: Senate −0.4 ± 1.2, House −1.1 ± 0.8, Governor −4.2 (−0.7 to −6.4 across folds). The sign is a retirement slump, and out-of-sample error does not improve.
Freshman-incumbent effect
Senate −0.9 ± 1.0, House −1.0 ± 0.7, Governor +4.4 driven by 2018, when Baker, Hogan and Scott had no record on file. No out-of-sample gain.
Prior-office quality prior
Net of the full money gap, holding prior office predicts nothing (state legislators −0.78 vs novices −0.77 as House challengers). The quality-challenger effect is expressed through receipts.
"Impute, never zero" money
Giving every race with missing receipts the structural gap under the raw basis raised error in all three offices.
Pollster-accuracy weights
Weighting polls by exp(−λ × rating score) moves no poll average at any strength; nor does subtracting a pollster's HISTORICAL house effect.
Dropping or shifting partisan polls
Costs House coverage (104 → 68 polled races) and error (3.68 → 4.16); shifting them 3 or 6 pts toward the opponent also hurts.
Demographic trend term
The nonwhite-share slope is real within a cycle but has flipped sign across cycles; kept as a simulation shock only.
Appointed-incumbent penalty
Appointees ran a mean 5.6 pts behind an open-seat generic, but on n = 9; a negative share would be an extrapolation. Share stays 0.
Special-election over-performance
Rejected as a metric by decision; not built.
Raw fundraising strip (k 0.02, cap 2) in the forecast
Still what TPL strips backward, but forward it was worse than no money term at all for the House.
OPEN ITEMS
Known limitations
House bias
The backtest shows House results running more Republican than predicted in 2022 and 2024 (pooled +1.34).
Governor
The structural model is weakest here (personal brands, cross-party governors), which is why polls carry Governor races fastest. Governor money k and the partial-cycle scale are borrowed from House/Senate, and 15 of the 2026 races have no state filings on file.
House backtest coverage
District presidential data is on 2026 lines only, so 2018 and 2020 contribute a handful of House rows; a wave-year district elasticity cannot be tested.
Environment history
Five even years identify c, s and the generic-ballot part of σ_E. The 2018–2024 generic-ballot values are the live recipe over the 538 archive; 2016 is RealClearPolitics' final (the archive starts in 2018), and RCP ran 1–2 pts more Republican than the recipe in 2022 and 2024, so that one row is on a slightly different basis.
Pre-2016 records
Candidate effects start in 2016, so long-tenured incumbents' earlier over-performance is invisible.
Poll freshness
2026 polls are scraped from Wikipedia and need periodic re-scraping; expert ratings and state-legislature track records are not inputs.

House races take the environment on the House-vote scale (HOUSE_ENV_ON_VOTE_SCALE): β* × E_hat ÷ s, national error without the × s

E is one environment fitted across every office, so it moves about s = 0.57 points per point of House popular vote: Senate, Governor and presidential margins swing less than the House vote does, and in 2022 the other way. The forecast converted the generic ballot to PV_hat (D+7.4 today) and then to E_hat (D+3.7), and House races received β* × E_hat — so the 435 House projections summed to a national House vote of D+4.9 (D+2.9 on contested seats) while the model's own House-vote estimate was D+7.4. Race polls: undecided voters split between the candidates, up to 10 points per poll
